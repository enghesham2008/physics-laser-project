What is a Laser & What Are Its Types?

Definition of LASER: Standing for "Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation," a laser is a device that emits a highly focused, coherent, and monochromatic beam of light. Unlike ordinary scattered light, laser waves travel in phase and in a single direction with extraordinary precision and energy.

Main Types of Lasers (By Gain Medium):

1. Solid-State Lasers

Use solid crystals doped with rare-earth ions, such as Ruby and Nd:YAG lasers. Widely applied in precision surgeries, dermatology, and heavy metal cutting.

2. Gas Lasers

Operate by passing an electric current through a gas medium like Helium-Neon (He-Ne) or Carbon Dioxide (CO₂). Known for high beam quality in metrology and industrial engraving.

3. Semiconductor (Diode) Lasers

Compact, ultra-efficient lasers that convert electrical signals into light pulses—serving as the high-speed engines of fiber-optic internet and global data centers.

A Groundbreaking Patent Breaking Speed Barriers

Transverse-Coupled-Cavity VCSEL Design (TCC-VCSEL)

US Patent 10,658,815 B1

The Engineering Challenge:
Conventional Vertical-Cavity Surface-Emitting Lasers (VCSELs) used for optical data transfer faced a physical modulation bandwidth bottleneck (below 50 GBaud), limiting how fast data could move between chips in modern computing.

Objective & Global Impact of the Patent:
This invention introduces ultra-short transverse-coupled feedback cavities adjacent to the main laser cavity. This triggers a Photon-Photon Resonance (PPR) effect that shatters traditional speed limits—extending modulation frequencies beyond 100 GHz. The result is dramatically faster on-chip optical data transmission, reduced latency, and lower energy consumption for next-generation hyperscale data centers.
